# Artifacts

Things on the island that explain one idea each. Walk up to the yellow ring and press Inspect; a found one turns green. The CLI learns about them through the progress code.

- not yet: **The cafe** (the terrace of the Hospitality Hub): client, server, protocol. You are the client, Rolinda is the server, the menu is the protocol. You send a request; she sends a response with a status code and a body. Every API on the internet is this cafe with more tables. See [[HTTP and APIs]], [[Building and consuming APIs]].
- not yet: **The fountain** (the fountain by the lake): a cache. The first cup takes a walk to the well. The fountain keeps a copy, so the second cup is instant. A cache is a remembered answer: fast, cheap, and wrong once the world moves on. Claude's prompt cache does the same for the start of your prompt. See [[HTTP and APIs]], [[Context window and prompts]], [[Cost, tokens and model choice]].
- not yet: **The well** (the stone well): a database. Pulling a bucket is a query. Without a rope marked in metres you go down the whole well; an index is that rope. Two buckets at once is a transaction: both come up, or neither. See [[SQL and DuckDB]], [[Data - files, schemas, warehouses]].
- not yet: **The lighthouse** (the lighthouse on the point): DNS, names to addresses. Ships know the harbour by name; packets need a number. The lighthouse is the resolver: it turns tpetedb.github.io into an address and remembers the answer for a while. It never carries you anywhere. See [[localhost and ports]], [[HTTP and APIs]].
- not yet: **The dock** (the harbour): containers and deployment. A crate packed here opens the same on every island: the code, its runtime and its libraries in one box. Build packs it, push ships it, run unloads it. That is why it works on the other machine too. See [[Docker and containers]], [[CI-CD and automation]].
- not yet: **The windmill** (the windmill on the hill): schedules and cron. The mill turns without you. A schedule is five fields (minute, hour, day, month, weekday) and a command; a hook is the same command fired by an event instead of a clock. Both are how agents work while you sleep. See [[Headless agents and scheduling]], [[CI-CD and automation]].
- not yet: **The balloon** (the hot-air balloon): the cloud, rented by the hour. The cloud is a computer you rent and never see. The meter runs while the balloon is up, whether you look at it or not; region, size, storage and traffic are four meters, not one. Landing it is the only way to stop paying. See [[Cloud and servers]], [[Cost, tokens and model choice]].
- not yet: **The mountain** (the mountain in the north): the stack, layer by layer. Hardware at the foot, then the operating system, the runtime, the libraries, your app, and the agent at the summit reading and writing all of it. Every layer stands on the one below; a bug can live on any of them. See [[Unix and the terminal]], [[Python]], [[Python libraries - what they are for]].
- not yet: **The market stall** (the stall by the Hub): an API and its documentation. The menu on the stall is the API: what you may ask for, in which words, and what comes back. Order without reading it and you get a 400. Show your key and you get the good stuff. See [[Building and consuming APIs]], [[Security and permissions]].
- not yet: **The bridge** (the bridge over the river): an integration, MCP. On the other bank lives a tool the agent does not have. The bridge has a rulebook: first ask what is over there, then call it by name with the arguments it expects. That rulebook is MCP, and every connector is one more bridge. See [[MCP (Model Context Protocol)]], [[Security and permissions]].
- not yet: **The factory** (the factory by the harbour): a data pipeline. Raw is what arrived, bronze keeps it as it came, silver cleans and types it, gold answers one question; each step reads the one before and writes its own table. A good pipeline is idempotent: a failed run leaves nothing half-written, and a rerun on the same input gives the same output with no duplicates. See [[Data - files, schemas, warehouses]], [[Repetitive tasks, reliably]], [[CI-CD and automation]].
- not yet: **The post office** (the post office on the south point): message queues and pub/sub. You drop a letter and walk away; the queue keeps it until every subscriber has read it, even one that was offline. Delivery is at least once, so a reader must cope with the same letter twice, and a letter nobody can read goes to the dead-letter shelf instead of being retried forever. See [[HTTP and APIs]], [[Headless agents and scheduling]], [[Hooks]].
- not yet: **The shop** (the shop on the main street): a package registry. uv add polars is a purchase: the shelf is PyPI, every version sits on it with a date, and the wish goes into pyproject.toml. The receipt is uv.lock, exact versions and checksums, so uv sync --frozen rebuilds the same environment on any machine; a yanked version stays on the shelf with a warning not to take it. See [[Python libraries - what they are for]], [[TOML in practice - pyproject.toml]], [[Semantic Versioning]].
- not yet: **The bank** (the bank behind the Hub): secrets and auth. A token is a key: whoever holds it is you, as far as the server can tell. It lives in .env or the keychain, never in a commit and never in a vault note; a leaked key is revoked the minute you know, and rotating keys on a schedule means a leak you did not notice dies on its own. See [[Security and permissions]], [[.env files and secrets]], [[Claude Code, the power settings]].
- not yet: **The data centre** (the hall next to the Hub): where the model runs. A request travels to the nearest edge, waits for a GPU node, reads your prompt in one pass and then types the answer one token at a time; most of the wait is the typing. Distance adds latency, a node that has to load the weights first adds a cold start, and batch jobs fill the GPUs cheaply because nobody is waiting. See [[Cloud and servers]], [[Open weights versus closed models, on your own Mac]], [[Kubernetes and platforms]].
- not yet: **The energy grid** (the pylon and the turbine by the harbour): compute and cost. Tokens are the watts: every request draws some in and some out, output costs more because it is generated, and the bill is the meter reading times the price per million. A rate limit is a fuse that trips before the wiring melts, autoscaling adds turbines when demand spikes, and a budget alarm rings before the invoice does. See [[Cost, tokens and model choice]], [[Cloud and servers]], [[Scaling laws and the Bitter Lesson]].
- not yet: **The library** (the library behind the Hub): RAG and embeddings. A question becomes a vector, a long list of numbers, and the notes that sit nearest to it in that space come back as the shelves to read; the model answers from those pages and cites them. The index is a snapshot: a note edited after the last build is invisible until you re-index, so the rebuild belongs on a hook. See [[Memory - the vault as long-term memory]], [[Context window and prompts]], [[Markdown and Obsidian]].
- not yet: **The office** (the office tower next to the Hub): a team of agents. One brief, three roles: a planner reads AGENTS.md and writes the steps, a worker edits and runs the tests, a reviewer reads the diff against the plan and the handbook and may say no. Subagents are departments, each with a fresh context and a narrow brief, reporting back in a paragraph so the main agent's context stays small. See [[Subagents and multi-agent]], [[AGENTS.md]], [[Hooks as gates]].
- not yet: **The households** (the row of houses on the west shore): users and privacy. Every row in a users table is a person and every column is something of theirs, held with their consent for a stated purpose. Collect only what the feature needs, share aggregates and hashes rather than rows, and design the delete before the insert, because a request to see or erase everything you hold must be answered within thirty days. See [[Security and permissions]], [[Data - files, schemas, warehouses]], [[SQL and DuckDB]].
- not yet: **The school** (the school behind the Hub): training a model. Data goes in, weights come out: the model is a very long list of numbers adjusted a little on every example until the loss stops falling. The data is split once into train and test, the exam the model never sees while learning, so a train score far above the test score means it memorised the homework; a benchmark is that exam shared by every model, one number that can be compared. See [[From base model to assistant]], [[Neurons and backprop (1943 to 1989)]], [[Evals - measure the agent, not the vibe]].
