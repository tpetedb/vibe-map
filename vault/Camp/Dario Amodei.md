# Dario Amodei

*CEO and co-founder, Anthropic*

Co-author of the 2020 scaling-laws paper, former VP of research at OpenAI, co-founded Anthropic in 2021 with a safety-first thesis. Wrote Core Views on AI Safety and the essay Machines of Loving Grace on what could go right.

**What they would tell you**
- Scaling is predictable; that is both the opportunity and the reason for caution.
- Interpretability and alignment research have to keep pace with capability.
- Be honest about uncertainty, in both directions.

**Going deeper**
The scaling-laws paper is where the power-law plots come from. Core Views explains why Anthropic trains frontier models at all while worrying about them. Machines of Loving Grace is the optimistic case, written to be argued with. Read both and you understand the company behind the tool you are using.

## The encounter
- Why does everyone expect more every year?
- Dario Amodei: Because the loss follows a power law in model size, dataset size and compute, measured over more than seven orders of magnitude. He is one of the authors of that paper. ([Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361))
- Predictable enough to plan on?
- Dario Amodei: Predictable enough that the plan is the easy part, which is exactly why the care is needed. ([Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361))
- And the risk?
- Dario Amodei: His company publishes three worlds rather than one: the optimistic, the intermediate where the problem is real but tractable, and the pessimistic. Safety work has to happen at the frontier because large models differ from small ones. ([Core Views on AI Safety](https://www.anthropic.com/news/core-views-on-ai-safety))
- Is that not hedging?
- Dario Amodei: It is stating the uncertainty in both directions instead of picking the convenient end of it. ([Core Views on AI Safety](https://www.anthropic.com/news/core-views-on-ai-safety))
