# Historical attribution review of three new origin sets

Reviewed independently on 2026-09-22 by `codex-audit-places-sources`. Scope: the new origin sets in `o-origins-agents`, `o-origins-data-engineering`, and `o-origins-data-net`. This review reads the Galaxy brief's requirement that a topic sits where its historical event happened. It does not repeat technical tests or issue a work-order ruling.

The earlier registry source review verifies organisation/city associations and marker coordinates only. It does not prove that every historical event involving an organisation happened at that place. In particular, a current registered address, an unrelated dated press release, a website footer, or an employer affiliation cannot silently become an invention-site claim.

## Actionable attribution gaps

Each cited page below was opened during this review. These are evidence gaps, not claims that the stated cities are historically false. They need resolution before the origins can be accepted under the Galaxy brief. An online announcement can use `the-internet`; a private development event cannot become an online event by changing only its place identifier. Keep the actor, date and actual milestone accurate.

## Stronger primary evidence found

- **Redshift, 2012:** The existing AWS What's New article does not identify Seattle. The [same-day Amazon press release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2012/11/amazon-web-services-announces-amazon-redshift) explicitly carries a Seattle dateline on 28 November 2012 and announces the same limited preview. Replace the origin source with this release and, if needed, describe the announcement as issued from Seattle. This supports the announcement city, not a claim that all Redshift engineering happened there.
- **Engelbart, 1962:** The existing [report transcription](https://dougengelbart.org/content/view/138) identifies SRI research but its extracted text does not expose Menlo Park. Engelbart's [1968 paper, reference 6c](https://dougengelbart.org/content/view/140/) explicitly identifies the exact October 1962 report as SRI, Menlo Park, California. This provides historical publication-site evidence and can accompany the report source. The scanned original was also opened, but its extracted cover text was incomplete; this review relies on the independently read 1968 reference, not an unseen image.
- **Kafka, 2011:** The [21 July invitation](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/archive/come-linkedin-hear-talk-about-kafka-our-open-source-distributed-pub-sub-messaging-system) explicitly locates a Kafka talk at LinkedIn's Mountain View headquarters on 27 July 2011. It is useful evidence for that talk and the named team at that time. It does not explicitly locate the 11 January release. Preserve either milestone with its own wording and provenance.
- **dbt, early history:** Tristan Handy's [first-person retrospective](https://www.getdbt.com/blog/of-the-community-by-the-community-for-the-community) places the career discussion preceding his company in Fishtown, Philadelphia. It also discusses the early 2016 dbt community. This supports that limited founding-story association. It does not explicitly place the data-quality feature release or every developer's work in Philadelphia. The page's modern last-edited date must not be used to resolve its relative “five years ago” wording without the original publication context.

## Existing Earth attributions with direct historical support

- `security` / MIT / 1975: The [paper metadata](https://web.mit.edu/Saltzer/www/publications/protection/Meta.html) identifies the manuscript dates and its authors' Project MAC/MIT affiliation in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The claim is about those MIT authors publishing that paper, not the exclusive birthplace of all security principles.
- `duckdblab` and `sql` / CWI Amsterdam / 2018: The [DuckDB Foundation](https://duckdb.foundation/) explicitly states that both named creators co-created DuckDB at CWI Amsterdam in 2018.
- `data` / IBM San Jose / 1970: The [IBM history](https://www.ibm.com/history/relational-database) explicitly places Codd at the IBM San Jose Research Lab in San Jose when discussing the 1970 paper.
- `data` / UC Berkeley / 1986: The [PostgreSQL history](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/history.html) identifies POSTGRES as written at UC Berkeley and dates implementation to 1986.
- `apis` / UC Irvine / 2000: The [dissertation abstract](https://ics.uci.edu/~fielding/pubs/dissertation/abstract.htm) explicitly identifies Fielding's dissertation at UC Irvine in 2000. This is an institutional dissertation milestone, not a claim that all REST ideas arose at one desk.
- `apis` / Amazon Seattle / 2002: The [Amazon release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2002/7/amazon-com-launches-web-services-developers-can-now-incorporate-amazon-com-content-and-features-into-their-own-web-sites-extends-welcome-mat-for-developers) is explicitly datelined Seattle, 16 July 2002. Preserve the claim as an announcement, not a development-location claim.
- `http` / CERN / 1990: The [Berners-Lee biography](https://www.w3.org/People/Berners-Lee/Longer.html) explicitly connects CERN in Geneva, his work there, and the server work begun in October 1990.
- `http` / UC Irvine / 1997: [RFC 2068](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2068) provides its January 1997 date and Fielding's contemporary UC Irvine address. The current wording names his contribution rather than assigning the entire multi-author standard to Irvine.
- `sql` / IBM San Jose / 1974: The [participant reunion transcript](https://www.mcjones.org/System_R/SQL_Reunion_95/sqlr95-System.html) places the System R/SEQUEL work at San Jose and includes the contemporary San Jose research-report reference. Its paper bibliography also distinguishes publication/conference sites from the research lab. Keep that distinction.

## Abstract origins and review limits

Existing abstract places (`the-internet`, `ietf`, `a-standards-body`, and `apache-software-foundation`) do not imply an Earth city and therefore do not present the geographical defect audited here. This is not a new full content review of their dates or claims. The already-online Dagster, dlt, JSON Lines and Polars entries can remain geographically abstract; their original source reviewers still own their exact event verification.

No product files, review rulings, tests or commits were changed. Builders should document which findings they resolve, and the reviewer should re-open any replacement source before accepting the changed historical claim.
